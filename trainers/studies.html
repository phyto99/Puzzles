A practical guide to running your own cognitive training study — from design to analysis. No lab required.

Study Design Framework

1

Define your question

Pick one exercise and one measurable outcome. Vague questions produce uninterpretable results.

Question template: "Does [X sessions/week] of [exercise] over [N weeks] improve [specific outcome] compared to [control condition] in [population]?" Example: "Does 4 sessions/week of EFT over 6 weeks improve Stroop interference scores compared to a passive control in adults aged 18–35?"
2

Choose your design

  • Within-subjects (A→B): Same person does pre-test, trains, does post-test. Simple, small N needed. Confound: practice effects.
  • Between-subjects (RCT): Random assignment to training vs control. Gold standard. Needs 2× the participants.
  • Active control: Control group does a different cognitive task (e.g. arithmetic instead of EFT). Rules out placebo/motivation effects.
  • Passive control: Control group does nothing. Easier to run, weaker evidence.
3

Pre-register your hypothesis

Write down your prediction before collecting data. Use OSF (osf.io) — free, takes 5 minutes. This prevents HARKing (Hypothesizing After Results are Known) and makes your study credible.

Pre-registration template (OSF): Title: [Study name] Hypothesis: [Training group will show X% improvement on Y measure] Primary outcome: [Specific test, e.g. Stroop interference in ms] Secondary outcomes: [e.g. N-back accuracy, self-reported focus] Sample size: [N per group, with power calculation if possible] Analysis plan: [paired t-test / ANCOVA / mixed ANOVA] Exclusion criteria: [e.g. <80% session completion]
4

Measurement battery

Use validated, free cognitive tests. Measure before and after training. Include at least one transfer measure (different from the trained task).

5

Training protocol

Consistency matters more than intensity. Based on the studies in this database:

  • Minimum effective dose: 3–4 sessions/week, 15–30 min each
  • Minimum duration: 4–6 weeks (most studies use 8–12)
  • Adaptive difficulty: tasks should stay at ~80% accuracy (not too easy, not too hard)
  • Log every session: date, duration, performance score, any notes
Session log template: Date | Exercise | Duration | Score/Accuracy | Level | Notes 2025-01-15 | EFT | 22 min | 84% | L3 | felt distracted 2025-01-17 | EFT | 25 min | 89% | L3 | better focus ...
6

Sample size

Use G*Power (free) or this rule of thumb:

  • Within-subjects, medium effect (d=0.5): N=27 for 80% power
  • Between-subjects, medium effect (d=0.5): N=52 per group for 80% power
  • Pilot study (proof of concept): N=10–15 is fine — just be honest about limitations

Most studies in this database used N=20–50. Smaller is fine for a personal or classroom study — just don't overclaim.

7

Analysis

You don't need SPSS. Use free tools:

  • JASP (jasp-stats.org) — free, GUI, Bayesian + frequentist
  • R + ggplot2 — free, reproducible, publication-quality
  • Google Sheets — fine for t-tests and basic stats
Basic analysis plan: 1. Check normality (Shapiro-Wilk) 2. Pre-test equivalence between groups (t-test or Mann-Whitney) 3. Primary analysis: paired t-test (within) or ANCOVA with pre-test as covariate (between) 4. Effect size: Cohen's d (small=0.2, medium=0.5, large=0.8) 5. Report: mean ± SD, t-statistic, p-value, 95% CI, Cohen's d 6. Visualize: box plots with individual data points (not just bar charts)
8

Report your results honestly

Whether positive or null, your results are valuable. Null results prevent others from wasting time. Report:

  • Exact p-values (not just "p<0.05")
  • Effect sizes with confidence intervals
  • Dropout rate and reasons
  • Any protocol deviations
  • Limitations (small N, no active control, self-selected sample, etc.)

Share on OSF, Reddit (r/cognitivescience), or submit to PLOS ONE (open access, accepts replications).

Quick-start: 6-week personal study

Pick one exercise. Take the Stroop test and digit span test (free online) as your pre-measures. Train 4×/week for 6 weeks, logging every session. Retest. Calculate Cohen's d. That's a real study. If you do it with 10+ people and pre-register, it's publishable.
