# Library Philosophy
*On the two systems and what they are for.*

---

## The Observatory

The Observatory is a library of elite external challenge sources — ranked, scored, filtered by 8 prestige criteria, used for deliberate cognitive training. It is evaluative and hierarchical. Every entry is scored 0–24. Rejection is explicit. The whole system serves the practitioner who is trying to get better at something hard.

It answers: *what is worth my serious attention, and when does it open?*

---

## Strat-archive

Strat-archive is a cabinet of wonders — curated by affect, not rigor. PyChess sits next to A Dark Room and Image Streaming protocols. There's no threshold. There's no score. The organizing principle is not "is this elite?" but "is this interesting, and what state of mind is it for?" It serves the practitioner who wants to wander and be surprised.

It answers: *what do I feel like doing right now, and what will change how I think?*

---

## The Gap

The Observatory's BAR criterion — barrier to entry — systemically filters out accessible resources. This is by design. A score of 0 on BAR means anyone can walk in. The Observatory is not interested in anyone-can-walk-in.

Strat-archive fills exactly the gap the Observatory can't fill without betraying its own criteria.

The Observatory cannot shelve Universal Paperclips next to the IMO. It cannot index A Dark Room alongside the WPF Grand Prix. Doing so would collapse the evaluative logic the whole system depends on.

But those things are real. They matter. They change people. They just belong somewhere else.

---

## What They Share

Both are atlases of cognitive experience.

The Observatory maps the frontier of human competitive puzzle culture — the annual summits, the century-old traditions, the archives that took decades to build. Strat-archive maps the frontier of interactive, playful, mind-altering software — the ARGs, the simulations, the tools that rewire how you reason.

They are two differently filtered slices of the same territory. The filter is the difference.

ConwayLife appears in the Observatory at 19/24. Game of Life appears in Strat-archive. They are the same phenomenon — one entry point is the competitive, open-problem frontier; the other is the five-minute afternoon wandering. Both are valid. Neither replaces the other.

---

## The Temporal Complement

The Observatory is about *when*: releasing in 3 days, annual ceremony, opens Thursday.
Strat-archive is about *what mood*: strange, deep, systematic, quick.

Together they answer the actual question a practitioner faces:
*what should I do with my cognitive energy right now?*

One consults the calendar. The other consults the feeling.

---

## The Metaphor That Holds

The Observatory is the gym schedule and championship calendar.

Strat-archive is the cabinet on the wall you walk past on the way to the gym — not training, but nourishment. You don't score it. You don't schedule it. You reach for it when something is pulling at you that the calendar can't satisfy.

The practitioner needs both. They should never be merged.

---

## Intimacy

The generator is the only private system of the three. It lives on your machine, tracks your specific history, and its output is personal — what it generates for you after six months is shaped entirely by your gaps, not anyone else's. The Observatory and Strat-archive are both public atlases — anyone could read them and find them valuable. But the generator is a mirror that only reflects you.

That asymmetry is interesting. The Observatory is a library anyone can walk into. Strat-archive is a cabinet anyone can browse. The generator is a training regime that exists nowhere except in the record of what you've already done.

This means the three systems form a progression not just of epistemic mode but of intimacy. Strat-archive is the most outward-facing — it's essentially a curated recommendation to a stranger. The Observatory is more specific — it assumes you're serious about competitive cognitive culture. The generator is the most inward — it assumes you've been at this long enough to have a measurable history worth responding to.

And the Practitioner Bridge is the hinge point where the most private system reaches out toward the most public one — where your personal entropy data whispers you're ready for this against the Observatory's public calendar. That's architecturally elegant and philosophically coherent in a way that doesn't need documentation to work. It just needs the systems to exist and point at each other.

Where does Strat-archive sit in that intimacy hierarchy? Closer to the Observatory than the generator — but with one important difference. The Observatory's value is cumulative; you return to it seasonally, for years. Strat-archive's most valuable entries are one-time encounters. A Dark Room is not a relationship. It's a collision. That makes Strat-archive the least intimate of the three — the most like a public space you pass through rather than live in.
